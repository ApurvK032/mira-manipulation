  rows have no final numbers. Their in-place checks had passed.

  tracker lost red on top: its best pose matched the depth on 88-91% of its pixels (95% is needed), so the
  check failed and the arm retried after the cubes were unstacked. Not a physical failure.

  (`runs/reliability/trial10_red_lost/`) shows the raw depth is right: red's top face reads 99-100 mm above
  the table. The error is in the poses of stacked cubes: red's centre comes out 2-3 mm low, green's up to
  3 mm high, and red's pose sits a few mm too close to the camera and a few degrees off in yaw (a thin strip
  along its right edge reads about 10 mm farther than the model). Poses that pass the check easily exist
  (depth 0.97-1.00): the tracker does not find them. Fix to try: a stack prior (top cube face-up at exactly
  50 mm above the bottom one, refine only x, y and yaw).
- Placement direction (top minus bottom, x/y) was not logged, so a calibration bias cannot be read off
  this run; later runs log it.

## After the run: close cubes (finger-clearance planner)

- The 8 cm spacing rule was replaced by a finger-clearance check (robot/grasp.py `GripperShape`,
  `plan_top_down(obstacles=...)`): the open gripper's meshes must stay 8 mm from the other cube from rest
  down to the grasp and back up, and the finger direction is chosen to keep it so. A cube partly hidden by
  the other is accepted when TRACKING with at least 50% in view.
- Real arm, red about 1-2 cm from green (centres 70 mm apart, green 67% in view behind red): fingers
  closed along 118 deg, 11.7 mm closest approach to green; stacked on the first attempt, 49 mm above green,
  5.4 mm off centre (x +5.4, y +0.2). Snapshots: `runs/reliability/close_test_{before,after}.jpg`.
- The planner waited about 90 s for the partly hidden green to count as still: its pose jitters about
  1 mm per frame, and the old 2 mm peak-to-peak test passed in 1% of 1.8 s windows. The stillness test now
  compares the medians of the window's thirds (`is_still`): 99% on the same log, and a 2 mm/s slide still
  reads as moving 94% of the time.
